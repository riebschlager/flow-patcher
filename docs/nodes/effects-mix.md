# Effects and mix

Audio → Audio, ending at the single Output. Design: "Node catalog".

| Node | What it does | Flow source | Milestone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reverb ★ | Convolution with a generated impulse: Decay (0.3–8 s, log), Damping, Predelay, Mix (modulatable), Variant | `music.js` (`AmbientAudio.initialize`: the impulse is built inline, 5 s of low-passed noise) | M2 (Space and Orbit modes) |
| Delay ★ | Tempo-synced filtered feedback delay, divisions from 1/64 to two bars (Transport input); free Time (0.01–4 s, log) with no transport; Feedback 0–0.42, Tone (200 Hz–20 kHz low-pass, log) and Mix modulatable | `timing.js` (divisions); the delay chain is inline in `music.js` (`AmbientAudio.initialize`, the main and gesture echoes; the 0.42 is the gesture echo's feedback) and `rhythm.js` (`PercussionSynth.connect`) | M2 (Space and Orbit modes) |
| Filter | Low-pass, high-pass or band-pass; cutoff (log-scaled, 20 Hz–20 kHz) and Q modulatable | New | M2 (Conduct mode) |
| Pan / Gain / Mixer | Basic routing and levels | New | M2 (Conduct and Orbit modes) |
| Output ★ | Limiter (unity gain below −9 dBFS), soft-knee peak guard, master level. Always exactly one per patch | `output.js`, adapted (see Reuse from Flow) | M1 |

## Rules for this family

- **Output is special.** Every patch has exactly one Output, and it can't be deleted. It enforces the peak guard and limiter whatever reaches it ([ADR 0011](../decisions/0011-runtime-safety-limits.md)). The graph compiler rejects a patch with zero or two Outputs.
- **Delay feedback is capped at 0.42** in the node, even when the knob is modulated past it.
- **Modulation.** Modulatable params ramp, never jump, so live edits don't click: a cabled param follows its Signal in `automate` (`paramAutomation`), a knob edit ramps in place through `liveParams` and `update` (`editRamp`) ([ADR 0031](../decisions/0031-audio-automation-and-in-place-edits.md)).
- **Frequencies and times are log-scaled.** Filter's cutoff (and Sampled Voice's), Reverb's Decay, and Delay's Time and Tone set `scale: 'log'`: the knob gives every octave the same travel, and a cable's depth into the param is in octaves, value = base × 2^(Σ depth × signal), clamped to the range. Depth 1 on a 0–1 Signal sweeps one octave up from the knob; 3.17 sweeps 300 Hz to 2.7 kHz. Ramps between grid points and knob edits stay linear in Hz ([ADR 0032](../decisions/0032-log-knobs-depth-in-octaves.md)).
- **Reverb's impulse** is Flow's: noise through a one-pole low-pass (prev = prev·a + n·(1 − a), a = Damping), under a (1 − t / Decay)^2.8 fade with a 20 ms fade-in, after Predelay of silence, one noise stream per channel so a mono input comes back wide. It is deterministic: `rngAt(seed, nodeId, 'impulse', variant, channel)`, never Math.random, so each Reverb in a patch has its own impulse, the same on every run, and the Variant knob picks another. It is scaled so low tones come back at their own level on average at any Decay or sample rate (unit energy times (1 − a) / (1 + a)); single pitches vary by up to about ±8 dB around that, as a noise impulse's response is uneven. Web Audio's own normalization is off: that one made the wet level grow with the square root of the length (measured on node-web-audio-api: 0.14 of the dry level at 0.3 s, 0.71 at 8 s). Mix is an equal-power insert mix (dry cos, wet sin), not Flow's separate send and dry levels.
- **Reverb edits.** Every knob is in `liveParams`. Mix ramps (`editRamp`) or follows its cable (`paramAutomation`). Decay, Damping, Predelay and Variant aren't modulatable (each value is a new impulse); an edit builds a new convolver and crossfades the input from the playing one to it over 50 ms, so the old tail rings out and the wet signal neither clicks nor drops out. A new impulse is built at most once per crossfade (50 ms of audio-clock time): edits that come quicker wait, and the next `automate` or `update` after the crossfade builds the latest one, so a knob drag builds about 20 impulses a second, not 60, and crossfades never overlap. A waiting impulse is built at the next `automate`'s t0, which is the scheduler's horizon (about 120 ms ahead, up to 1.25 s while the tab is hidden), so during a drag each later edit lands about a lookahead late. At most four convolvers ring at once, plus the oldest fading out on its return for 50 ms when a fifth starts. Tails are disconnected once silent, checked in `automate` and `update` against the audio clock (no timers). After a rebuild of the Reverb alone (a cable into Mix) the old instance rings until its longest tail is over (`idleAt`).
- **Delay's time** is the division's beats over the exact tempo on its Transport cable (`transportTempo`, ADR 0034), rate included, so it follows a tempo change from the moment it happens; Flow's 21 divisions (`timing.js`), a dotted eighth by default and for an unknown value. With no transport (or a reading with no usable tempo) it plays the Time knob in seconds, and the Transport input shows "No transport: free time in seconds". The time is clamped to 32 s, Flow's ceiling: Flow clamped the tempo to 30–300 bpm instead, and Clock goes slower. The delay line is always automated on the 60 Hz grid, since the tempo can change without any edit. A tempo change glides linearly over 40 ms from the reading's `since`, starting where the delay was just before it (mid-glide when a modulated tempo's ticks come quicker than that), as a pure function of t. A Transport cable added or removed in a swap keeps the instance (only param cables rebuild), so switching between the transport and free time glides 40 ms too, from the grid point before the switch.
- **Delay edits.** Every knob is in `liveParams`. A Division or Time edit holds the old time until the swap's re-run is past the ramps it keeps (`GLIDE_LEAD`, two grid steps plus the 15 ms render margin, about 48 ms), then glides linearly over 40 ms (Flow's `delayGlide`, which was a `setTargetAtTime` time constant). The glide is part of the delay time as a function of t, so re-runs and rewinds read it alike. It never clicks, but a long jump bends the echo's pitch while it glides, as Flow's did (1/8 to 1/4 at 120 bpm plays the echo back at −5.25× for 40 ms). Feedback, Tone and Mix ramp (`editRamp`) or follow their cables. After a rebuild of the Delay alone (a cable into a param) the old instance echoes on until its repeats are 60 dB down (`idleAt`: 9 repeats at 0.42, the first echo included).
- **Delay's chain** is Flow's gesture echo: delay → low-pass (Tone, Web Audio's default Q) → feedback gain → back into the delay, with the wet signal taken after the low-pass, so even the first repeat is filtered. Feedback's knob stops at 0.42, and the node caps it at 0.42 again in `automate` and `update`, so no cable or edit gets past it. Mix is equal-power, as on Reverb.
- **Tails after a rebuild: a limitation.** A tail survives a rebuild only when the effect is rebuilt alone: its upstream moves to the new instance at the swap, and `idleAt` counts from the last time the effect was automated. When the upstream is retired in the same swap (a seed change rebuilds every audio node; Delay → Reverb with a seed change mid-echo), the old upstream keeps feeding the old effect past the effect's `idleAt`, and the effect is disposed while its tail is still audible. Fixing it needs the runtime to retire a chain together (follow-up).
- **Audio is the exception to "no cycles".** Audio cables don't carry events, so audio feedback loops go through Web Audio's own delay handling. Don't route them through the event cycle checker.
